# Concepts: inventing the idea for this reel

A concept is the idea a reel hangs on: what the viewer sees first, how the middle moves, how it ends, and
the one moment they remember. It comes before any look. Two reels with the same concept feel the same
whatever their colours, so the concept is where variety starts.

## How to find concepts for a brief

Start from the product, not from the list below. Ask:

- **What is its core gesture?** Dragging, sending, drawing, typing, scanning, booking, sorting. A reel
  built on that gesture (the whole film is one long drag; every scene change is a "send") belongs to this
  product alone.
- **What does its data look like?** Messages, files, shapes, numbers, maps, calendars, code. Make the
  data the hero: a calendar that fills itself, a chart that becomes the title, files that become confetti.
- **Whose day does it change, and when?** A precise moment (the 8:59 standup, the checkout queue, the
  3 a.m. bug) beats a generic "busy person".
- **What is the before?** The clumsy old way, shown honestly and briefly (no competitor branding).
- **What would a viewer screenshot?** A number, a line, a frame. Build towards it.
- **What does it feel like to use?** Calm, fast, playful, powerful, private. The concept should *feel*
  like that before a word is read.

Then write three concepts that differ in kind (not three variations of one). Check them against
`fingerprint.py fresh`: a concept type used in the last few reels should only come back if the person
asks for it.

## Concept types (starting points to transform, not slots to fill)

| Type | The shape | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| problem-solution | Pain, turn, the product fixing it, the ask. The classic; earn it with a sharp pain. | One clear pain, simple fix |
| cold-open-demo | Open mid-use on the product's best moment, no intro; name it at the end. | Products whose UI is the wow |
| manifesto | Statements on the beat, the product as proof between them. | Products with a point of view |
| ui-pov | The whole reel *is* the screen: cursor, taps, scrolls, in one take, no outside graphics. | Tools, dashboards, editors |
| mock-ad | A parody of another format: an infomercial, a weather report, a movie trailer, a nature doc, a recipe card. | Playful brands, crowded categories |
| day-in-life | A clock moves through a day; one use per moment. | Everyday consumer apps |
| data-story | A number or chart leads; the product explains it. | Analytics, finance, health |
| speedrun | A timer, a task done fast, the finish time (true, measured). | "It's fast" products |
| before-after | Split or swap between the old way and the new. | Replacing a clumsy workflow |
| chat-thread | The story told as a message thread, a group chat, comments or a support ticket. | Social, collaboration, support |
| quiz | Questions the viewer answers in their head; the product answers on screen. | Products with FAQs people really ask |
| countdown | "3 things", "5 seconds", a list that counts down to the ask. | Several equal features |
| metaphor | One physical metaphor carries the reel (a lock, a paper plane, a tidy desk, a garden). | Abstract products (security, sync, AI) |
| letter | A note to the user, written or typed on screen, read in time with the music. | Personal, warm products |
| kinetic-poem | Pure type, a few words a beat, the product only at the end. | Brand moments, launches |
| behind-the-scenes | How it works inside: the pipes, the packets, the model, drawn simply. | Developer tools, infrastructure |
| user-story | One invented person's small win, start to finish. | Consumer products, marketplaces |
| map-journey | A path across a map, a screen, or a timeline; the product moves along it. | Delivery, travel, sync, workflows |
| unboxing | The product revealed like a physical box: layers, lids, tissue paper. | Launches, new versions |
| split-compare | Two panels running at once: with and without, two users, two devices. | Sync, collaboration, comparisons |
| loop | The ending flows into the first frame so the reel replays seamlessly. | Short reels, social feeds |
| challenge | The reel dares the viewer: "find the bug", "spot the difference", "do this in 10 s". | Engagement, playful tools |
| recipe | Ingredients, steps, the dish: the product as a method. | Templates, workflows, builders |
| trailer | Movie-trailer grammar: title cards, a voice, a hit, "this summer". | Big launches, games |

Combine and bend them: a chat-thread *as* a speedrun, a manifesto told as a loop, a recipe card for a
data story. The type names exist so `fingerprint.py` can tell concepts apart; record the closest one in
the direction's `concept`.

## Length and format follow the concept

- 6-10 s: a single idea, a bumper, a loop. 12-20 s: one story, three beats at most. 20-30 s: a full arc.
  30-60 s: a walkthrough, only when the brief needs it (people drop off fast).
- 9:16 for Reels / TikTok / Shorts, 4:5 for feeds, 1:1 for mixed placements, 16:9 for YouTube, sites and
  presentations. The brief's platform decides; when it doesn't, let the concept choose and vary it from
  recent reels.

## Writing a concept up (what to show the person)

For each of the three:

```
A. <Title> (<type>, <format>, <length>)
   Logline: <one sentence>
   Opens on: <the first second, as an image>
   Middle: <how it moves, 1-2 lines>
   Ends on: <the last frame and the ask>
   Signature: <the moment people remember>
   Sound: <music genre or silence + the SFX character>
```

Make the three truly different: different type, opening, feeling and sound. If two could swap their
loglines, replace one.
